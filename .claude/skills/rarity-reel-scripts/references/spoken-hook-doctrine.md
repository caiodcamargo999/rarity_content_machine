# Spoken hook doctrine — the five triggers, said out loud

Same five triggers as the `rarity-ig-idea-engine` headline hook doctrine. The mechanism is identical;
only the delivery changes, because a spoken first line has to survive being heard once at normal
talking speed, not read and re-read on a slide. Pick ONE per script.

## The five triggers

**CONTRARIAN** — says the opposite of what the audience assumes, in Caio's own voice.
- Written (IG headline): "Most founders file the pop-up as a stunt, never as a channel."
- Spoken: "Everyone thinks popups are a stunt. I think they're the cheapest customer acquisition
  channel most DTC brands never try."

**AUTHORITY/ANTI-HERO** — a credible, specific claim of hard-won experience, stated plainly, not
boasted.
- Spoken: "I've looked at probably two hundred ad accounts. The ones losing money all make the exact
  same mistake."

**INNOVATIVE IDEA** — names a real mechanism or approach most people haven't heard framed that way.
- Spoken: "There's a number most agencies never show you. It's called contribution margin, and it's
  the only number that matters in Q4."

**APPARENT NONSENSE** — a line that sounds wrong or confusing for a beat, resolved by what follows.
- Spoken: "We made more money by turning off our best-performing ad. Let me explain."

**EXTREME CURIOSITY** — opens a loop a viewer has to keep watching to close.
- Spoken: "A client asked me to kill their highest-ROAS campaign last week. Here's why I said yes."

## What changes between written and spoken delivery
- **Shorter is more forgiving on a slide than in speech.** A spoken hook can run a full sentence or
  two, because the pacing and emphasis of a real voice carries more information than static text can.
  Don't force it into 6-9 words the way an IG headline is forced; aim for roughly 2-4 seconds of
  natural speech (6-12 words is a reasonable range, not a hard ceiling).
- **No italic/accent-word marking.** That's a visual device for the rendered art; it doesn't exist in
  speech. If a word needs emphasis, either structure the sentence so the emphasis is obvious from
  word order, or note it in the script as the word Caio should naturally stress when he says it.
- **The open loop subhead doesn't exist as a separate line here.** In the IG pipeline the headline and
  subhead are two visually distinct lines. In a spoken script, the hook and the next beat usually run
  together as continuous speech — the "subhead" job (promise the payoff without explaining it) is
  handled by the second sentence of the script, not a separate formatted element.
- **Deliverability check:** say the hook line out loud before finalizing. If you stumble on it, or it
  needs a breath in an awkward place, rewrite it — a hook that reads cleanly on paper can still be
  genuinely hard to say naturally.
