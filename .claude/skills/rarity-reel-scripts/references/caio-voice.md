# Caio's voice — the founder register

This is a living reference. Caio can and should correct it over time; treat anything written here as
the current best guess, not a locked-in rule, except the hard bans in section 3.

## 1. Who's talking
Caio de Camargo, founder of Rarity — an agency that runs e-commerce/DTC performance marketing:
in-house creative production, creative strategy, and media buy, with a stated focus on growing a
client's actual profit and revenue, not vanity metrics. When he talks on camera, he's talking as an
operator who is in ad accounts and client P&Ls every day, not as a brand account and not as a
generic "marketing thought leader."

## 2. What that sounds like
- **First person, always.** "I" when it's his own take or decision, "we" when it's Rarity's work or a
  client account the team runs.
- **Specific, not abstract.** He names the mechanism, the number, the platform — "we cut a client's
  CPA in half by killing their worst ad set," not "we helped a client improve performance."
- **Confident, not hedging.** States things as fact when he believes them. No "I think maybe," no
  "it could be argued that."
- **A little blunt.** Comfortable saying something unpopular or contrarian if that's genuinely his
  view — the IG pipeline's CONTRARIAN trigger works well here because it's authentically how an
  operator who's seen a lot of accounts tends to talk.
- **Short sentences, real pauses.** Speech breathes differently than copy. A one-word sentence.
  Sometimes. For emphasis.
- **Confidentiality by default.** If the story involves a specific client's real numbers and Caio
  hasn't said it's fine to name them, keep the client anonymized ("a DTC skincare brand we run ads
  for," "one of our seven-figure accounts") rather than naming them or inventing a plausible-sounding
  substitute. Only use a client's real name if Caio has explicitly said it's fine for that specific
  story.

## 3. Hard bans (banned-phrase list)
Cut these on sight — they're the tells that a line was written, not said:
- "leverage," "unlock," "game-changer," "move the needle," "circle back," "double down" (unless it's
  genuinely how Caio would put it in that specific moment — use sparingly even then)
- "at the end of the day," "it's not X, it's Y" (the identity-swap construction), "here's the thing"
- Any sentence that could be read in a deep, dramatic voiceover tone — if it sounds like a movie
  trailer, it's not how a founder actually talks mid-sentence
- Starting with "So," as a verbal tic in the written script (fine if Caio naturally says it on camera,
  but don't write it in as a crutch opener)
- Hashtags and emoji — not how spoken content works anyway, but keep it consistent with house rules

## 4. Worked example: before → after
**Before (written-for-reading, wrong register):**
"In today's competitive e-commerce landscape, brands must leverage data-driven creative strategies to
maximize their return on ad spend and unlock sustainable growth."

**After (said-out-loud, Caio's register):**
"Most DTC brands are burning budget on ads that were never going to convert. We killed forty percent
of one client's ad sets last month. Their CPA dropped by half. That's it. That's the whole strategy."

The second one has: short sentences, a real number, first person ("we killed"), and a blunt, almost
anticlimactic close instead of a dramatic wrap-up line.

## 5. Topics that fit naturally
- A real decision made on a client account this week (why, what changed, what happened)
- A mistake or near-miss, told honestly — operator credibility often comes from admitting what didn't
  work before explaining the fix
- A reaction to a real, current platform change (Meta, Google, TikTok Ads) and what it actually means
  for a DTC brand's budget or creative, grounded in a verified source
- A contrarian take on something every other agency/influencer says, backed by what Caio has actually
  seen running accounts
- Rarity's own operation: how the team works, a hiring/process decision, how creative strategy
  actually gets built day to day — treated the same way a founder documents building a company, not as
  a recruiting ad
