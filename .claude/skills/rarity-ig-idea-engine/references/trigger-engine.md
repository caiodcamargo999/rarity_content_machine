# Trigger Engine — finding the real anchor

Content is "non-generalist" only if it stands on something real. This file is how you find and verify
that anchor with live web research, and how you rotate so the feed stays varied.

## The rule
**Never write from memory alone.** Facts, numbers, dates and quotes drift. Run a web search, confirm
the specific detail, and keep the source URL. If you can't verify it, pick a different anchor.

## Anchor types (pick from a mix)

1. **On-this-day / anniversary**
   - Birthdays, deaths, debut/launch dates of icons and brands; anniversaries of famous campaigns,
     products, races, deals, IPOs.
   - First, get today's real date (it matters for "on this day" framing), then search e.g.
     `"[today's date] anniversary marketing OR business OR motorsport"`, `"on this day [Month Day] brand history"`.
   - Strong because it gives a reason to post *today*.

2. **Current news / cultural moment**
   - A brand's new campaign or rebrand, a viral moment, an earnings or funding milestone, a sports
     result, a product launch, a notable failure.
   - Search e.g. `"marketing campaign this week"`, `"brand rebrand 2026"`, `"[sport] result this weekend"`,
     `"startup funding announcement this week"`. Prefer the last 7–30 days for "news" anchors.

3. **Evergreen icon decode** (no time peg needed)
   - A legendary figure, brand, or moment with a documented story worth decoding. Modern: Ayrton Senna,
     Steve Jobs, Ferrari, Nike, Red Bull, Coca-Cola, Apple, Rolex, Kobe, Ronaldo, Lewis Hamilton.
   - **Historical figures are encouraged**, not only current ones: classic copywriters (Eugene Schwartz,
     David Ogilvy, Gary Halbert, Claude Hopkins, John Caples, Bill Bernbach), athletes (Muhammad Ali),
     philosophers and ancient stories (Aristotle, the Stoics, Sun Tzu), inventors and founders. Any true
     story that maps to a marketing, branding, business, or mindset idea is fair game.
   - The bar: the history must connect to ONE usable lesson for Rarity's audience. Decode the idea, not trivia.
   - Use when Caio wants quality over timeliness, or to fill a batch. Still verify the specific fact.

## How to verify (quality bar)
- Confirm the *specific* detail you'll build the post on (the number, the quote, the decision), not
  just the general topic.
- Prefer primary or reputable secondary sources. Be skeptical of viral "facts" — many famous business
  anecdotes are exaggerated or apocryphal. If sources conflict, soften the claim or pick another.
- Quotes: attribute exactly and verify wording. A misattributed quote kills credibility for an agency.
- Keep ≥1 source URL per idea in the brief.

## Choosing the subject when Caio gives no topic
Bias toward subjects that:
- Rarity's audience already admires (founders, brands, athletes, iconic companies) — admiration makes
  the decode land.
- Have a *documented, specific* behavior to decode (a decision, a number, a method) — not vibes.
- Carry transferable business/branding/mindset lessons (so they fit a pillar cleanly).

Avoid: politically charged figures/events, tragedies used as a hook, anything that reads as
opportunistic. When decoding a figure who died (e.g. Senna), keep it respectful — honor the standard,
don't exploit the death.

## Rotation logic (for batches)
When producing multiple ideas:
- Rotate the **pillar** so it never repeats back-to-back (Growth → Business → Mindset → Growth…).
- Vary the **anchor type** (don't stack three dead-icon posts; mix in a news hook and a brand decode).
- Vary the **format** (don't ship five carousels in a row; intersperse statics).
- Vary the **subject domain** (sport, tech, fashion, F&B, luxury) so the grid feels curated.

## Output of this stage
For each idea you should be able to fill, in the brief:
- Subject (who/what), the verified fact (with number/date/quote), source URL(s), and the time-peg
  (on-this-day / news / evergreen). Then hand to the Decode Method in the editorial playbook.
