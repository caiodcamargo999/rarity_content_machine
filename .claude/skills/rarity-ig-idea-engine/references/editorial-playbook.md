## 1. The reference DNA

**@brandsdecoded__** (AI content agency, ~300k): decodes brands and the marketing/AI world into
clean, punchy carousels. Strong hook slide, one idea, confident tone, designed to be saved & shared.

**@blankschoolbr** ("we form the next generation of CMOs", ~215k): sharp, social-native marketing
education. Hot takes, frameworks, "if you do X you're doing it wrong", high opinion density.

**What we borrow:** the *decode* instinct, the one-idea discipline, the hook-first structure, the
confident-teacher tone, save-worthy carousels.

**What we change:** we anchor every post to something *real and specific* (a named person, brand,
moment, number) instead of generic advice — and we run it through Rarity's premium, systems-driven
voice. Less "10 tips", more "here's the mechanism behind a thing you already admire."

## 2. The Decode Method

Every post is the same three-move move:

1. **THE REAL THING** — a concrete, sourced fact. What actually happened / what they actually did.
   (e.g. "Senna would drive qualifying laps so far beyond the car's limit that engineers thought the
   telemetry was broken.")
2. **THE INSIGHT** — the non-obvious mechanism underneath it. The part most people miss.
   (e.g. "He wasn't chasing the lap time. He was redefining what the team believed was possible — and
   the whole garage rose to it.")
3. **THE TAKEAWAY** — what Rarity's audience does differently. Specific, usable, today.
   (e.g. "Your brand's ceiling is the standard you refuse to lower. Set the impossible number first;
   the system reorganizes around it.")

The decode is the product. The image and headline are just the delivery.

## 3. The three pillars

Rotate across these. Same anchor, different extraction.

### Growth Marketing & Branding
Ads, positioning, demand creation, authority, offers, distribution, scarcity, pricing-as-message.
- *Worked angle (Ferrari):* They cap supply below demand on purpose → "Demand is manufactured, not
  found. The waitlist IS the marketing."
- *Worked angle (Liquid Death):* Sold water by selling an identity → "Don't sell the product. Sell
  the enemy your customer wants to beat."
- *Worked angle (Eugene Schwartz, copywriter):* "You cannot create desire, only channel it." → "Stop inventing demand. Enter the conversation already in your prospect's head."

### Business & Strategy
Scaling, pricing, moats, bets, business models, decisions under pressure, focus.
- *Worked angle (Netflix killing DVDs):* Cannibalized their own cash cow → "The bet that scares your
  CFO is usually the one that saves the company."
- *Worked angle (Amazon Day 1):* "Day 2 is death." → "Treat every quarter like the brand is brand new."
- *Worked angle (Aristotle):* "We are what we repeatedly do." → "Your brand is not your slogan. It is the action you repeat in public."

### Mindset & High Performance
Discipline, standards, obsession, the cost of greatness, preparation, identity. The Senna lane.
- *Worked angle (Senna):* Relentless standard-setting → "Talent gets you noticed. Standards make you
  untouchable."
- *Worked angle (Kobe 4am):* Obsessive practice → "The work nobody sees is the gap nobody closes."
- *Worked angle (Muhammad Ali):* He called himself "the greatest" before he was. → "Declare the standard out loud first. Identity drags performance up to meet it."

> Pillar tagging guides the design step's color mood too (see the designer skill): Growth/Branding →
> teal-accent energy; Business/Strategy → deep navy authority; Mindset/Performance → gradient drama.

## 4. Headline patterns (the ONE line on the image)

The headline is the only text on the hero photo. 3–7 words. It must stop the scroll.

- **The flip:** take the obvious and invert it. "Talent is overrated." / "Scarcity sells more than ads."
- **The cost line:** name the price of the thing. "Greatness has a receipt."
- **The number-shock:** a real, surprising figure. "1 second. 14 years of work."
- **The naming:** name the mechanism people feel but can't articulate. "This is manufactured demand."
- **The challenge:** dare the viewer. "Most brands would never do this."
- **The quote-flip:** a real quote, reframed for the audience. (Attribute correctly in the caption.)

Rules: no fluff words ("ultimate", "secret", "hack"), no emojis, sentence case
(the headline is an editorial serif with one italic accent word in magenta; the designer renders the accent).

**Accent for impact:** the headline is a white editorial serif; the emphasis is ONE italic word in
magenta (mark it with *asterisks* in the brief). That italic accent is what stops the scroll, not an
off-brand poster color. Everything stays on-brand: the photo, the symbol, the teal eyebrow, the serif.

**No dashes:** headlines (and captions) carry no hyphens or dashes. Use periods, commas, or colons.

## 5. Hook doctrine (slide 1 / first caption line) — MANDATORY on every carousel

Every carousel's slide 1 is a HOOK built on exactly ONE of these five triggers. Name the trigger in
the brief's "Hook trigger" field; if the headline fits none of them, it is not done.

1. **CONTRARIAN** — attack the common belief head on.
   "You can't create desire." · "Stop paying for free sales." · "Great copy isn't clever."
2. **AUTHORITY / ANTI-HERO** — the icon who broke the rule and won, framed as a paradox.
   "The anti-party: married, sober, worth millions." · "He gave the product away and got rich."
3. **INNOVATIVE IDEA** — a new mechanism named like a discovery.
   "Meta trained a brain on your ads." · "Your creative is the targeting now."
4. **APPARENT NONSENSE** — a line that seems wrong or absurd, and only makes sense as the
   carousel unfolds. "Claude is terrible at content. Paste this prompt anyway." ·
   "She got the prediction wrong and went viral because of it."
5. **EXTREME CURIOSITY** — an open loop that is almost physically hard not to tap.
   "The most stolen book in library history is a marketing manual."

**Structure of the hook slide (reference: @vinci.society, @brandsdecoded__):**
- The HEADLINE = the trigger line, 3 to 9 words, no explanation.
- ONE open loop subhead under it: a short question or subversion that promises the payoff and
  points into the carousel ("Why does his discipline offend people, but print millions? →").
  Write it in the brief's "Open loop subhead" field, EN + ES + PT. End it with an arrow.
- The hook NEVER explains. Explanation lives in slides 2 to N. Never bait without a payoff:
  the body must cash the exact check the hook writes.

Test before shipping: read the headline alone. If a stranger would not feel a pull to swipe,
rewrite it with a harder version of the same trigger. "Interesting" is not enough; it must itch.

See `headline-engine.md` for the data-backed lift patterns, emotional-trigger checklist, and
rejection checklist to pressure-test any of the five triggers above before it ships.

## 6. Carousel architecture (6–10 slides)

1. **Hook slide** — shock-value hero image + trigger headline + open loop subhead (see section 5).
2. **Tension / setup** — frame the real situation or the common belief you're about to break.
3–7. **The decode** — one beat per slide. The real thing → the insight → why it matters. Big serif
     number/word per slide, one short BentonSans line. No walls of text.
8. **The takeaway** — the single transferable lesson, stated plainly.
9. **CTA slide** — Rarity logo, a line ("Let's build something rare."), follow/DM prompt, handle.

Keep one idea per slide. If a slide has two ideas, split it.

## 7. Caption structure & Rarity voice

**Structure:**
- Line 1: the hook (re-stated or sharpened from the headline).
- 2–4 short paragraphs: the decode (real thing → insight → takeaway). Short sentences. White space.
- CTA: a clear next step — "Save this." / "Send it to a founder who needs it." / "DM 'RARE' to talk
  growth." / "Follow @rarity.agency for the decode."
- Hashtags: 3 maximum, fewer is better (even zero). One branded (#RarityAgency) plus one or two sharp, relevant tags. Localize per language.

**Rarity voice (from the brand):**
- Confident, premium, systematic. "We build marketing that creates momentum."
- Short declaratives. "Growth is not luck. It is a system."
- A little contrarian, never arrogant. Speaks to ambitious founders and brands.
- Signature closers: "Let's build something rare." / "You bring the vision. We build the engine."
- Avoid: guru clichés, hype words, fake urgency, emoji spam, generic motivation.

## 8. Do / Don't

**Do**
- Anchor to a real, verifiable fact with a source.
- Give the headline a real hook and a pop color (off-brand is fine) so it stops the scroll.
- One big idea. One headline. One image subject.
- Make the Spanish and Portuguese versions *land*, not just translate.
- Recommend static vs carousel with a reason.

**Don't**
- Write "tips" with no anchor.
- Use hyphens or dashes in headlines or captions. Reword with periods, commas, or colons.
- Pile on hashtags. Three maximum, often fewer.
- Claim numbers/quotes you didn't verify.
- Put more than the headline on the hero photo.
- Reuse the same pillar back-to-back in a batch.
